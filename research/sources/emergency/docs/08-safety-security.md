# 8. Safety & Security

Source: U.S. State Dept South Korea country page [S1]; US Embassy [S2].

## Travel advisory level: **1 — Exercise normal precautions** [S1]
- This is the **lowest** of the 4 levels — the same as many Western
  countries. South Korea is generally very safe, including for solo/paired
  travelers, nightlife, and public transit.
- Two things the advisory flags:
  1. **Large-scale demonstrations** can occur with little notice and may
     disrupt transport/services. Avoid gatherings if you see one forming.
  2. **Tensions on the Korean Peninsula remain high.** North Korea conducts
     missile launches and military activity; South Korea runs civil
     emergency drills throughout the year. Monitor local news and **any
     government Emergency Alert texts** to your phone.

## Enroll in STEP (Smart Traveler Enrollment Program) [S5]
- Register at **https://step.state.gov** so the U.S. Embassy can reach you
  with alerts and help locate you in an emergency. **Do this before you fly.**

## Practical safety guidance
- **Crime:** Petty theft/pickpocketing is low but not zero, especially in
  crowded nightlife (Itaewon, Hongdae) and markets. Use an RFID-blocking
  passport wallet; don't leave bags unattended.
- **Crowds:** Be crowd-safety aware in dense nightlife districts (see the
  Itaewon 2022 note in `06-holidays-traffic-dates.md`). Avoid compressed,
  bottlenecked alleys.
- **Demos/politics:** Keep away from protest areas; don't photograph
  sensitive sites (military/government).
- **Traffic/roads:** Korea drives on the **right** (like the U.S.). Crosswalks
  and signals are standard, but drivers sometimes turn through pedestrians —
  look both ways. If you rent a car, an **International Driving Permit** is
  needed along with your U.S. license.
- **Scams:** Common tourist-targeted scams are rare but use the 1330 helpline
  and **Tourist Police** (via 112/1330) for complaints. Beware overpriced
  taxis or "shops" that pressure large purchases.

## Medical / hospital safety
- Large hospitals with international clinics are the best choice for
  emergencies (see `03`). Keep insurance receipts.

## Lost passport / emergency while in Korea
1. Call the **U.S. Embassy Seoul 24/7: +82-2-397-4114**. [S2]
2. In a police/life-safety emergency, call **112** (police) or **119**
   (ambulance).
3. For lost/stolen passport, the Embassy can issue an emergency passport to
   allow you to fly home. Bring any ID/photos and your passport copy
   (`02`).
4. U.S. State Dept is reachable 24/7 from abroad at **+1-202-501-4444**. [S1]

## Civil emergency drills
- South Korea periodically conducts **civil-defense / air-raid drills**
  (sirens, phone alerts). Treat as a drill unless told otherwise; follow the
  guidance in any English-language alert or ask hotel staff. [S1]
