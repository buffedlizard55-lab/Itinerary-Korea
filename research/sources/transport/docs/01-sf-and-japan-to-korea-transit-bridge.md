# From San Francisco & Japan to Korea: The Ultimate Transit Bridge Guide

Welcome to South Korea! As travelers with experience navigating **San Francisco (Muni, BART, Caltrain, Uber)** and **Japan (Suica, JR Pass, Shinkansen, Tokyo Metro)**, you already possess a strong foundation in public transit. However, South Korea has its own distinct, highly advanced transit rules, etiquette, and payment systems.

This guide bridges your existing mental models from SF and Japan directly to South Korea's transit ecosystem.

## 2. Why Google Maps Fails in South Korea (and What to Use Instead)

In San Francisco and Japan, **Google Maps** is your best friend. In South Korea, **Google Maps walking and transit navigation is severely restricted** due to Korean national security laws that prevent domestic mapping data from being exported to overseas servers.

* **What happens if you use Google Maps in Seoul?** It will not give you walking directions between subway exits, and bus schedules can be outdated or incomplete.
* **The Solution:** Download **[Naver Map](https://map.naver.com)** (iOS App Store: `id311867728`) or **[KakaoMap](https://map.kakao.com)** before departing. Both offer full English interfaces, ultra-precise subway car/door boarding recommendations, and live bus countdowns.

## 3. The 30-Minute Free Transfer Rule: Why You MUST Tap Off

In San Francisco's Muni system, your Clipper tap gives you a 2-hour transfer window automatically. In Japan, you tap on and off at station gates, but transferring between different bus and subway companies usually costs a full second fare.

In South Korea, you get **free or deeply discounted transfers** between subways and buses (and bus-to-bus on different numbered lines) under one golden rule:
1. **You must tap your T-money / WOWPASS card when BOARDING (`승차`).**
2. **You must tap your card again when ALIGHTING (`하차`) from buses AND subways.**
3. Your transfer must occur within **30 minutes** (extended to 60 minutes between 21:00 and 07:00).
4. **What happens if you forget to tap off a bus?** You will be charged the maximum route penalty fare on your next ride, and you will lose your free transfer!

## 4. Payment Realities: Apple Pay vs. Physical Cards

* **In San Francisco & Japan:** You can add a Clipper card or Suica/Pasmo directly to Apple Wallet on your iPhone and tap your phone at turnstiles.
* **In South Korea (2026):** **Apple Pay cannot be used for subway or bus transit turnstiles.** While Apple Pay works at convenience stores and some retail chains via NFC, South Korea's transit network uses a proprietary Korean IC protocol.
* **What You Need:** Every traveler must carry a **physical T-money card** (3,000 KRW at any convenience store) or a **WOWPASS card** (which combines a T-money IC chip with a foreigner debit card).

## 5. Transit Etiquette: South Korea vs. Japan vs. SF

1. **Priority Seats (`노약자석` - Pink & Yellow seats):**
   * *In SF:* People sit in priority seats and stand up when an elderly or disabled passenger boards.
   * *In Korea & Japan:* **Never sit in the designated priority seats at the ends of subway cars**, even if the train is completely empty! There are also pink "Pregnant Women Priority Seats" (`임산부 배려석`)—leave them vacant.
2. **Quiet on Trains:**
   * Like Japan, phone conversations and loud talking on subways and buses are considered rude. Set your iPhone to silent mode (`매너모드`).
3. **Escalator Etiquette:**
   * **Stand on the RIGHT, walk on the LEFT** (identical to Tokyo/SF, opposite of Osaka).
4. **Food & Drink:**
   * *On Subways:* Closed bottles are fine; open food is discouraged.
   * *On City Buses:* **Strict law in Seoul:** Bus drivers will legally refuse boarding if you are carrying open takeaway coffee cups or unsealed food containers! Carry a sealed bottle or finish your drink before boarding.
